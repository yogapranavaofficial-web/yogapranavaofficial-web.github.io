The name

What "Pranava" means

Pranava is the Sanskrit name for Om — described in the yogic texts as the first sound, the vibration underneath all others. It isn't a symbol you chant at the start of class and forget; it's a reminder that yoga begins with sound and breath before it ever becomes a shape on a mat.

The studio takes its name from that idea. Every session — however simple the postures — starts by returning to breath first.

What's offered

Four ways to begin

आ

Asana

Traditional postures held with attention — building strength, flexibility and balance at a pace that suits the body in front of her, not a fixed routine.

ध्या

Meditation

Guided sitting practice to quiet a busy mind. Useful on its own, and often taught alongside postures to close a session.

प्रा

Pranayama

Breathing techniques at the heart of the practice — the "Pranava" in the studio's name. Learn to use breath deliberately, not just notice it.

क्रि

Kriya

Cleansing and energizing techniques that prepare the body and mind for deeper practice — traditionally paired with pranayama and meditation.

Why yoga, why here

A practice, not a performance

Yoga Pranava isn't built around difficulty or flexibility as a goal. It's built around consistency — showing up, breathing properly, and letting the body change on its own timeline.

Classes are held in person and kept small, so there's real attention on form and breath rather than a room full of people following along at a distance.

"Yoga does not begin with the pose. It begins with the breath that carries you into it."
